# P-003 — Project Governance & Construction Oversight

## Public portfolio purpose

This case study documents an ongoing management-control approach for parallel construction and facilities workstreams in education organizations. The portfolio focus is project governance rather than technical construction design.

## Current verified public claims

- Oversight currently represents two education sites.
- Five-plus parallel construction/facilities workstreams are represented in the current case-study scope.
- Three-plus cross-work dependencies have been explicitly surfaced for management follow-up.
- Vendor payments are monitored in stages alongside delivery or work progress.
- The underlying projects remain in active execution and monitoring.
- Field updates are translated into current project status and significant project-history records.
- Significant records can include decisions, people involved, issues/risks, actions, results/follow-up, lessons, and next review points.

## Public narrative

**Problem:** Parallel workstreams had different vendors, dependencies, payment stages, progress conditions, and unresolved decisions. A simple “in progress” label was not sufficient for management oversight.

**Approach:** Convert field updates into a repeatable cycle:

1. Capture update
2. Classify significance
3. Update current project status
4. Log significant project history
5. Track dependencies
6. Review management decisions
7. Follow through until the item changes state or closes

**Current outcome:** Improved management visibility and traceability across parallel workstreams. Final construction schedule, cost, and completion outcomes are not yet claimed.

## Sanitization rules

Do not publish:

- vendor identities
- exact project locations beyond broad organizational context
- quotation values or negotiated target values
- payment amounts
- bank/payment evidence
- private contact information
- internal disputes or personal commentary

Use generalized workstream labels such as interior fit-out, electrical, external works, and structural package when illustrating the control logic.

## Update triggers

Update the same case study when one of these becomes verifiable:

- a major workstream reaches completion
- a blocked dependency is resolved
- a meaningful vendor/scope decision is closed
- operational use of the project-control workflow expands
- final cost/schedule outcomes become available
- a measurable management benefit can be supported by evidence

The project should remain one evolving case study rather than being split into separate “design,” “implementation,” or “improvement” portfolio items.
